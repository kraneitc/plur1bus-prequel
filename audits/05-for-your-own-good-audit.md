5. Yoon offers custody plus a supervised channel; Rios chooses communications and transport cuts before the next interval.
6. The cuts degrade local care without changing Joined coordination, confirming the collective’s forecast.

8. Thousands of harmless authorized actions reveal an operation distributed beyond known sites and without a single disabling node.
9. Omar’s refusal to let two terminals absorb an avoidable excess makes acceleration safer and more viable.
10. Epidemiology proves the perimeter was always a detection boundary, not the network’s edge.
11. Mara shows that captured personnel continuously expose Rios’s own operation through assigned work and ordinary records.
12. Rios stands down and restores systems, but the collective treats her demonstrated reach as a permanent future risk.
13. Deshpande’s truthful refusal removes Mara’s fantasy that her voluntary exposure could purchase a return to consent.
14. Tom’s ordinary memory and a grocery worker’s job access identify Mara’s order; the new policy turns domestic care into nonconsensual exposure.
15. Mara preserves the scarce intervention, then cannot destroy the original samples without crushing Jenn’s hand.
16. Rios and Mara each refuse an indiscriminate protective act, leaving functioning infrastructure available to carry acceleration.

**Result:** Rios’s doctrine does not mechanically cause acceleration. Its competent success changes the collective’s risk calculation, after which the collective knowingly chooses the consent breach.

## Consent and truth pass

- Yoon’s supervised-channel proposal preserves custody and medical evaluation while leaving civilian coordination intact. Rios rejects it before the network abandons assent.
- Jenn and Deshpande answer direct questions without euphemism: acceleration is nonconsensual, projected deaths reach hundreds of millions, uncertainty is wide, and the model has no unit for autonomy.
- Mercer’s Stage 3 “no” remains an individual refusal. His earlier intention to converge does not authorize exposure of anyone else.
- Omar explicitly refuses the broader operation while correcting an excess. Genuine network assistance and biological pressure do not erase his separate statement.
- Sofia’s recorded refusal remains factually valid. Joined Mel and Jenn state that policy will not honor it as an exemption; they do not pretend the old promise still controls conduct.
- Deshpande truthfully states that voluntary exposure by Mara or the original team will not stop acceleration. He distinguishes model output from future fact and admits the chosen course may kill more people if the model is wrong.
- The peaceful orientation protocol is not retroactively called false. Its expressly disclosed limit on future collective control becomes morally inadequate once policy changes.
- Mara’s prepared-food exposure occurs only after acceleration abandons assent. The scene identifies the broad route and policy while withholding preparation, amount, and handling.

## Character and relationship pass

- Rios runs a humane, competent containment operation and then mistakes measurable local interruption for proof of a clean protective path.
- She rejects Yoon’s pause because her private need makes intact coordination look like surrender, not because she forgets shielding has failed.
- Her stand-down is operationally real. Mateo’s response shows that correcting the perimeter does not repair the family pattern of turning contact into reporting.
- Mara keeps the season spine through three irreversible recognitions: her science helps Rios name the threat, her body cannot bargain for consent, and Tom’s remembered care can now locate her.
- Deshpande’s exact language remains a form of care and control after convergence. He surrenders every rhetorical defense without changing the policy.
- Omar preserves the distinction between preventing excess and endorsing the act, then experiences relief that makes the distinction harder to maintain.
- Tom’s green-lid memory is trivial enough to be intimate and specific enough to become leverage. His voluntary Joining does not authorize what the collective does with it.
- Jenn uses Mara’s known aversion to injury without striking or threatening her; the nonviolent act still coerces.
- Sofia remains offscreen but materially present through the no-contact form whose promise the collective remembers and breaks.

- **Potential intelligence failure:** Rios knows outages cannot block telemetry and uses them only to stop ordinary compromised actions; Yoon objects; analysts demand corroboration; Omar withholds implementation detail; Sato refuses unsupported routes; Rios later stands down.

- **Long consequence:** The consent breach and distributed plan drive Part Six; Omar’s correction affects casualty scale; Mara’s exposure and staged stock create her intervention; preserved samples and Jenn’s hand establish that the network knows and uses moral limits.
- **Memorable images:** Detainees sitting before entry; every face turning toward a dead speaker; a red marker inaccessible behind the communications lock; the perimeter reaching the wall edge twice; clocks stopped at eleven above moving departures; the wrong green lid; **STOPS NEW BUILD**; Jenn’s hand under steel; parallel headings.
