# agents.md

## Scope

These instructions apply to the entire `Plur1bus Prequel` workspace.

This is a collaborative fan-fiction development project for *Pluribus: Before We Were Us*. Preserve the user’s creative authority. Do not continue drafting beyond the deliverable explicitly approved in `progress.md` or the user’s latest message.

## Source-of-truth order

When instructions conflict, use this order:

1. The user’s latest explicit instruction.
2. This `agents.md` file.
3. `progress.md`, including its approved next action and decision log.
4. `season-rules-and-ledgers.md` for scientific, telemetry, knowledge, truth, consent, and causality rules.
5. `pluribus-prequel-treatment.md` for the season arc.
6. `gilligan-quality-writing-guide.md` for craft standards.
7. Existing prose in `manuscript/`, which remains exploratory unless explicitly accepted in `progress.md`.

Do not silently resolve a material contradiction. Record it under **Open decisions** in `progress.md` and stop before the contradiction would meaningfully alter new story material.

## Spoiler boundary

- The permitted canon boundary is *Pluribus* Season 1 through Episode 4.
- Do not use information from Episode 5 onward, even if it appears in search results, general knowledge, recaps, filenames, or incidental context.
- Do not foreshadow later-season answers accidentally.
- If outside research is needed, restrict research to scientific sources and official or recap material covering Episodes 1–4.
- Record any new canon facts that materially affect the project in `progress.md` before incorporating them.

## Style boundary

- Write original prose. Do not imitate Vince Gilligan’s exact wording, dialogue patterns, or distinctive surface style.
- Target high-level craft qualities: exact causality, visual storytelling, patient process, competent characters, dry situational humor, moral ambiguity, and consequences that persist.
- Avoid generic hive-mind villain language, secret alien malice, unearned speeches, and anonymous catastrophe used as shorthand for emotion.
- Professional language should be accurate, but the physical action and stakes must remain understandable.
- Once a character fully converges, do not enter that body’s private viewpoint again.

## Mandatory workflow

Before writing or revising story prose:

1. Read `progress.md` completely.
2. Read the relevant sections of `season-rules-and-ledgers.md` and `pluribus-prequel-treatment.md`.
3. Read the relevant quality gates in `gilligan-quality-writing-guide.md`.
4. Confirm that the requested work matches the approved next action.

Proceed through these gates:

1. Freeze creative rules.
2. Break all six episodes at scene level.
3. Draft and revise one representative sample.
4. Draft one approved episode at a time.
5. Perform a season-wide continuity and style pass.

Do not draft the complete season in one uninterrupted pass unless the user explicitly asks to change this workflow.

## Story rules that must not drift

- Infection is staged over days. Shaking is not instant conversion.
- S-78 transmission requires a plausible biological exposure; brief touch is insufficient.
- The RNA encodes a molecular interface, not a hive mind.
- Infected neurons and glia manufacture the S-78 transceiver complex.
- The brain remains the processor and participates in a nonlocal shared state.
- Distance and ordinary electromagnetic shielding do not block telemetry.
- No central hive or transmitter exists to destroy.
- Uninfected people cannot perceive the network, and the network cannot remotely read them.
- The network knows only what connected hosts know, perceive, remember, or access through ordinary means.
- Fully Joined speakers cannot knowingly assert a falsehood, but truthfulness is not omniscience or complete disclosure.
- During the peaceful month, deliberate exposure requires disclosed risk and active assent. Acceleration abandons this rule and is the season’s decisive moral breach.
- Preserve ambiguity about the sender’s motives, immunity, reversibility, and whether memory continuity equals personal continuity.

If any requested scene requires breaking one of these rules, flag the change before writing the scene.

## Continuity safeguards

- Mel—not Jenn—brings the doughnuts for his fourteen-year-old daughter’s marching-band fundraiser.
- Jenn is bitten by the rat at 3:36 a.m.
- The first month of spread is peaceful and produces no deaths.
- Military discovery triggers acceleration.
- The canonical death count referenced through Episode 2 is 886,477,591.
- The antisense intervention must be planted before the finale and cannot dismantle an already manufactured neural interface.
- Faraday cages, jammers, bunkers, and telecommunications outages may test the telemetry but cannot interrupt it.

## Quality gates

Every episode outline and prose draft must demonstrate:

- a decisive turn caused by character choice rather than coincidence;
- competent action based on the information available;
- a process the reader can understand well enough to fear a deviation;
- a cost carried by a named person;
- material, institutional, relational, or interior residue that survives the episode;
- at least one plausible exit from disaster that a character closes for an understandable reason;
- a final image containing both success and loss.

Do not polish dialogue before causality and rules survive review.

## File conventions

- All file and folder names use lowercase words separated by dashes.
- Episode outlines belong in `outlines/` and use `nn-title-outline.md`.
- Prose belongs in `manuscript/` and uses `nn-title.md`.
- Do not overwrite exploratory prose when making a substantially different approach; create a clearly named revision or obtain user approval to replace it.
- Update `progress.md` after every material change to rules, approvals, outline status, or manuscript status.
- Preserve unrelated user changes and untracked files.

## Completion rule

A deliverable is not “approved” merely because it exists. Only the user can approve treatment decisions, outlines, prose samples, episodes, or the assembled season. Record explicit approval in `progress.md`.
