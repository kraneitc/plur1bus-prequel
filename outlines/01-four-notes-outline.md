# Part One — Four Notes

## Episode architecture

- **Owner:** Mara Velez
- **Governing question:** Can the signal be biological?
- **Concrete want:** Determine what the four-tone sequence encodes and keep the work in competent hands.
- **Private need:** Make uncertainty yield before it can expose the limits of her expertise.
- **Irreversible choice:** Mara defeats the observation-only protocol and wins authorization to synthesize S-78.
- **Cost bearer:** Jenn first; the world later. Tom begins paying the relational cost immediately.
- **Episode clock:** The government briefing at the end of Mara's first day, followed by a forty-eight-hour authorization window.
- **Final image:** Four pipettes descend toward four wells.

## Scene outline

### 1. The printer works

- **POV / alignment:** Observatory printer technician; cold open.
- **Place / time:** Radio observatory operations room, dawn, the morning after discovery.
- **First image:** Four toner cartridges stand open beside hundreds of pages marked in four repeating colours.
- **Want and process:** The technician clears a mundane paper jam while astronomers manually mark each recurrence of the 78-second signal and argue about whether the printer has shifted the colours.
- **Turn / choice:** The printer test page is perfect. The colour order belongs to the signal, not the machine. The astronomers keep feeding paper rather than call public affairs.
- **Residue:** A physical stack establishes repetition, the four-symbol alphabet, and the first informal decision to contain the news.
- **Last image / next cause:** The repaired printer idles unnoticed while another four-mark sequence emerges; the lead astronomer calls for a biochemist.

### 2. A noun changes the room

- **POV / alignment:** Mara.
- **Place / time:** Mara's university office and the observatory video link, morning.
- **First image:** Mara mutes a student presentation while the signal pulses silently in a second window.
- **Want and process:** She tests the astronomers' language hypothesis against spacing, recurrence, and the four-symbol constraint, asking for raw captures rather than their colour transcription.
- **Turn / choice:** She recognizes that the pattern behaves like a biochemical alphabet and says "nucleotide" before she has decided whether she should.
- **Residue:** The observation converts an astronomy curiosity into a potential biological instruction set and fixes Mara as the person who named the danger.
- **Last image / next cause:** Every face in the video grid goes still; an administrator reaches across the camera and disconnects the public network cable.

### 3. The first handoff

- **POV / alignment:** Mara.
- **Place / time:** Observatory conference room, late morning.
- **First image:** Visitors' phones accumulate in padded evidence envelopes normally used for damaged telescope drives.
- **Want and process:** Mara needs the unaltered data before classification swallows it. An intelligence liaison needs a reproducible chain of custody before letting any sample leave the room.
- **Turn / choice:** Mara catches a transcription error in the liaison's packet and earns access by reconstructing its origin from the raw capture.
- **Residue:** She gains the full sequence but accepts the first secrecy agreement; the correct data now sits inside a government-controlled record.
- **Last image / next cause:** Her signature appears beneath a paragraph she does not finish reading because Deshpande is waiting on a secure line.

### 4. Unknown family

- **POV / alignment:** Mara.
- **Place / time:** Provisional biology review room, afternoon.
- **First image:** Deshpande has covered the word **virus** on a whiteboard with a strip of tape labelled **NOUN PENDING**.
- **Want and process:** Mara and Deshpande compare the sequence's conceptual architecture with known biological categories while explicitly separating observation from construction.
- **Turn / choice:** Deshpande shows that it does not fit a known family. Mara shows that it nevertheless contains organized, non-random instruction-like regions. Neither claim makes synthesis necessary.
- **Residue:** Their shared conclusion—biological information of unknown type—triggers mandatory biosafety and national-security reviews.
- **Last image / next cause:** A meeting invitation titled **FOREIGN BIOLOGICAL MATERIAL** arrives, despite there being no material yet.

### 5. One ordinary dinner

- **POV / alignment:** Mara.
- **Place / time:** Mara and Tom's kitchen, evening.
- **First image:** Tom serves two plates while Mara's secure phone occupies the third place at the table.
- **Want and process:** Tom wants a truthful reason she has cancelled their weekend. Mara tries to preserve both the clearance and the appearance of intimacy.
- **Turn / choice:** He asks whether the work is dangerous. She answers only that the laboratory is not doing anything, a technically accurate statement that avoids what she is advocating.
- **Residue:** Tom accepts the answer but begins an ordinary domestic ledger of unexplained absences; Mara learns that narrow truth can still be a betrayal.
- **Last image / next cause:** In bed, Tom sleeps while Mara listens through an earpiece to the signal repeat every 78 seconds.

### 6. Observation only

- **POV / alignment:** Mara.
- **Place / time:** Interagency biosafety conference, next morning.
- **First image:** A risk officer places two folders on the table: a thin one marked **OBSERVE** and a thick one marked **CONSTRUCT**.
- **Want and process:** The panel builds an observation-only protocol: monitor the broadcast, compare independent captures, prohibit synthesis, and circulate only derived findings.
- **Turn / choice:** Deshpande supports the cautious plan because construction would create the only known biological sample. Mara cannot refute that fact.
- **Plausible exit:** The project can remain observational indefinitely; no living risk yet exists.
- **Residue:** Mara loses the first vote and is given forty-eight hours to submit a minority argument before the protocol is signed.
- **Last image / next cause:** The **OBSERVE** folder closes over Mara's notes, forcing her to prove why inaction is also a choice.

### 7. The sky is an unsecured archive

- **POV / alignment:** Mara.
- **Place / time:** Temporary secure workroom, day two.
- **First image:** A world map fills with radio observatories capable of receiving the same patch of sky.
- **Want and process:** Mara traces what information is already recoverable outside the classified project and distinguishes control of this team from control of the broadcast.
- **Turn / choice:** An astronomer confirms that several non-government instruments have partial captures. Mara frames the risk: another group can reconstruct the sequence without their safety controls.
- **Residue:** She gains a defensible synthesis argument but also creates a dissemination assessment that intelligence officials can use to widen classification.
- **Last image / next cause:** Deshpande circles one sentence—"controlled knowledge may be safer than uncontrolled first use"—and agrees to help prosecute it.

### 8. Design the cage before the animal

- **POV / alignment:** Mara.
- **Place / time:** Invented maximum-containment suite, day two.
- **First image:** Jenn walks an empty transport carrier through a chain of doors while observers time each badge release.
- **Want and process:** Mara, Deshpande, Jenn, and the safety officer make the proposed containment process legible: custody, sealed transfers, independent verification, stop conditions, and destruction authority. No construction method appears.
- **Turn / choice:** Jenn identifies a handoff that leaves one technician alone with both the material and the incident log. Mara revises the protocol and gives technicians independent stop authority.
- **Residue:** Jenn becomes the named person who will physically execute historic work and therefore the first human cost if the safeguards fail.
- **Last image / next cause:** The empty carrier returns clean; the safety officer stamps the procedure **FEASIBLE**, not **SAFE**.

### 9. Jurisdiction

- **POV / alignment:** Mara.
- **Place / time:** Authorization hearing, day three.
- **First image:** Six agencies have made six different title slides for the same signal.
- **Want and process:** Mara must answer whether synthesis produces knowledge unavailable through observation. Opponents press containment, unknown function, and the moral difference between receiving information and embodying it.
- **Turn / choice:** A senior official proposes waiting for a multinational accord, a second plausible exit. Mara argues that delay redistributes the risk to an unknown laboratory without reducing it.
- **Choice under pressure:** Asked whether she can guarantee safety, Mara says no, then insists uncertainty is the reason to learn under controls they can audit.
- **Residue:** The panel approves conditional synthesis. Her honest uncertainty wins trust while her need to govern uncertainty wins the dangerous precedent.
- **Last image / next cause:** Six electronic signatures begin routing through a document whose project title is wrong.

### 10. The wrong title

- **POV / alignment:** Mara.
- **Place / time:** Secure corridor outside the suite, late afternoon.
- **First image:** The authorization PDF reads **PROJECT CHORUS** although every earlier document says **FOUR NOTES**.
- **Want and process:** Jenn refuses to open the suite until the mismatch is resolved. Mara wants to avoid losing the scheduled containment window but follows Jenn's stop authority.
- **Turn / choice:** The error is clerical, not sinister. Correcting it costs hours and proves that the procedure can halt even under prestige pressure.
- **Residue:** Jenn trusts Mara for backing her; Mara receives the scientific respect she wanted from Deshpande when he supports the delay without taking credit.
- **Last image / next cause:** The corrected authorization prints one page at a time behind wired glass.

### 11. What Tom cannot be told

- **POV / alignment:** Mara.
- **Place / time:** Parking structure outside the facility, night.
- **First image:** Mara watches Tom's missed-call notification disappear beneath a containment countdown.
- **Want and process:** She tries to call him before work begins but cannot answer the only question that matters: what are you making?
- **Turn / choice:** She sends a bland promise to be home tomorrow and returns inside when the countdown pauses for her badge.
- **Residue:** Scientific control succeeds by spending relational trust; the marriage's classified absence becomes a future vulnerability rather than background colour.
- **Last image / next cause:** Her badge opens the final door while Tom's reply—**Okay. Be safe.**—remains unread.

### 12. Four wells

- **POV / alignment:** Mara, with Jenn carrying the physical action.
- **Place / time:** Containment suite, night.
- **First image:** Four pipette tips wait above four empty wells; every human hand remains outside the robot enclosure.
- **Want and process:** Jenn reads the authorization identifier, Mara verifies the stop conditions, and Deshpande confirms the observation log. The construction itself remains outside the frame.
- **Turn / choice:** Mara is offered the final abort control. She lets the validated sequence continue.
- **Immediate success:** The institution performs exactly as designed; nothing breaks, alarms, or behaves strangely.
- **Residue / recognition:** Mara has converted an unowned signal into material for which named people are responsible.
- **Final image:** The four pipettes descend in perfect unison. Cut before they touch the wells.

## Episode gate check

- **Two visible exits closed:** Observation-only monitoring; delay for an international accord.
- **Legible procedure:** Signal validation, custody, authorization, containment rehearsal, and final go/no-go.
- **Choice creates turn:** Mara's synthesis argument—not an accident—creates the viable sample.
- **Continuing residue:** Samples and protocol; classification precedent; Jenn's stop authority; Deshpande's respect; Tom's accumulating exclusion.
- **Rules audit:** No reproducible construction detail; no claims about telemetry or transmission before evidence exists.
- **Success and loss in final image:** Flawless coordinated work creates the first physical doorway to catastrophe.
